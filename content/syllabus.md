## Course description

Builds upon Audio Production II. Instructs in advanced multi-track editing
techniques, including advanced audio mixing, signal processing, and critical
listening. Also covers business practices for the music industry and career
preparation, including copyright law, resume building, and online music
marketing and promotion.

In practice this section is a spatial audio course. It begins with acoustics and
the physical properties of sound, then works through stereo, binaural,
ambisonic, and multichannel formats, using both historical and contemporary
spatial audio technologies. Technical instruction in recording, mixing, REAPER,
and the Ambisonic Toolkit runs alongside critical listening, collaborative
critique, and the spatial aesthetics of music, sound art, and cinema.

**Prerequisite:** DAD 322. **Credits:** 3.

## Learning outcomes

By the end of the course, students will be able to:

- Describe the history and application of immersive sound technology
- Describe the different aspects of spatial audio psychoacoustics — how sound affects spatial perception
- Apply that knowledge of spatial audio psychoacoustics to create immersive audio sound designs
- Demonstrate proficiency in producing sound designs for multi-channel speaker systems using ambisonics and Dolby Atmos

## Major topics

- **Sound and space** — physical attributes of acoustic waves, space and time in music and sound art
- **Psychoacoustics** — distance perception, localisation, spatial impression
- **Stereo** — reproduction theory and the standard microphone techniques
- **Binaural** — HRTFs, SOFA files, recording and rendering with Anaglyph
- **Ambisonics** — theory, recording, and mixing with the Ambisonic Toolkit in REAPER
- **Surround** — cinema sound history, 5.1 and 7.1 aesthetics, ReaSurroundPan
- **Dolby Atmos** — object versus channel-based audio, mixing and delivery in Logic Pro

## Semester at a glance

The [schedule](/schedule/) has the day-by-day plan. In broad strokes:

- **Weeks 1–2** — Acoustics, space and time in music and sound art, historical context; the mono sound walk (Project 1)
- **Weeks 3–4** — Psychoacoustics; stereo reproduction and recording; binaural with the Zoom H3-VR and Anaglyph (Project 2)
- **Weeks 5–6** — Ambisonics: theory, recording, and mixing with the Ambisonic Toolkit (Project 3)
- **Weeks 6–8** — Cinema sound history; 5.1 and 7.1 aesthetics; ReaSurroundPan
- **Weeks 8–11** — Dolby Atmos in Logic Pro (Project 4); commercial and hybrid spatial tools
- **Weeks 12–16** — Final projects: proposals, work sessions, midpoint critique, and the exam-slot presentation

## Course structure

- **Lectures** — theory, listening, and demonstrations
- **Labs** — hands-on work in the classroom, the studios, and the immersive room
- **Projects** — four graded projects plus a self-directed final project
- **Critique** — projects are played out loud in class and discussed as a group

  a 50-point attendance pot for each additional absence (see the attendance

## Attendance and make-up policy

Students are expected to attend all scheduled classes. After three absences, 10
points will be deducted from the 50-point attendance pot for each additional
absence.

Late work will only be accepted with a documented excuse. Students may redo work
they received an undesirable grade on once, but only if they turned in something
by the original due date. The deadline for redoing work is one week after the
original due date. After this time, no late work will be accepted.

## AI tools

Audio software increasingly ships with AI features, and they aren't all alike.
Tools that process your own recordings, such as stem separation or noise
reduction, are fine to use in this class; mention them in your submission notes
the same way you'd name a sample source. Tools that generate the creative
material itself, such as loop or full-song generators, don't belong in graded
work unless an assignment says otherwise. When you're not sure which side of
the line a tool falls on, ask before you submit. If this territory interests
you, DAD 492 (Topics: AI and Music) spends a whole semester on it.
