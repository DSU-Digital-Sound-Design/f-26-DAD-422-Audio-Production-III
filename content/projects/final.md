## Your task

Create a new, finished **Ambisonics or Dolby Atmos** piece that shows what you can do
with sound and space. Choose music, a soundscape, an electroacoustic work, or
sound design for picture. Make deliberate choices about what the listener
notices, where sounds seem to be, and how the experience develops.

Aim for **3–5 minutes**. A shorter complete piece or self-contained film excerpt
may be approved in your proposal when its scope offers enough challenge and
room for development. Agree on the scope before production. A longer piece is
not automatically a stronger project.

The proposal is worth **30 points** and the final project **100 points**.
Everyone works toward the shared quality standards below, with individual
creative and learning goals agreed in the proposal. You do not need to learn a
new application; developing your listening and mixing decisions in familiar
tools can be a substantial challenge.

## Proposal: November 23

Bring your initial ideas and questions to the **November 18 final project discussion**.
Submit the following written proposal to D2L by class on **November 23**, ready to
discuss your plan.
**No prototype or finished audio is required with the proposal.**

1. **Title and description, 1–2 paragraphs.** Name the format, source material,
   intended duration, and concept. Describe the experience you want the listener
   to have. Include an influence and explain one specific connection to your work.
2. **Three goals and how you will evaluate them.** State one artistic goal, one
   technical skill to develop beyond earlier work, and one listening experiment
   that will help you make a decision. Describe what you will listen for or show
   as evidence for each goal.
3. **Scope and production plan.** Identify what you will compose, record, edit,
   or mix yourself; the materials you already have; and the work still needed.
   Plan toward the dates below, including access to the immersive room. Agree
   with me on your master format and playback setup.
4. **Sources and intended sharing.** List the creators and sources of supplied
   music, film, recordings, or samples. Bring the relevant license or permission
   information so we can check whether the finished work can be shared publicly,
   privately, or only in class. Choose a source suitable for your portfolio plans.

For a listening experiment, you might compare two treatments of distance in the
same passage. Ask a listener which better supports the scene, then use what
you hear and their feedback to choose or revise the treatment. A well-reasoned
decision to keep the original is valid evidence too.

The proposal earns its 30 points for completing these four elements
thoughtfully. It is not graded on having produced audio or a "perfect" idea. We will use feedback to agree on feasible, worthwhile goals.

The **November 30 prototype** is a rough excerpt of about 30–60 seconds that
tests your central spatial idea and routing. It can use temporary sources; it
does not need to represent the whole piece. We will use class time to work
through problems.

A **complete rough mix** covers your entire approved duration, including the
ending. It can have unfinished balance, transitions, and spatial details; it
should let us hear how the whole piece works.

At critique, listeners give one timestamped observation and one actionable
suggestion tied to the creator's question. Record the feedback and make at
least one substantive revision after critique. Explain what changed and why.
You do not have to follow every suggestion.

These milestones are feedback opportunities, not additional separately scored
assignments. Their evidence supports the final assessment of experimentation
and revision. Book room access early. Our room uses the verified **7.1 layout**
and has no discrete height speakers; include headphone checks when evaluating
height choices.

## Shared quality standards

- **Intentional spatial design.** Placement, distance, width, movement, and
  stillness support the musical or narrative focus. Explain specific decisions
  using spatial perception concepts from the course. More motion or more
  complicated routing does not automatically improve the piece.
- **A coherent, controlled mix.** Essential material stays clear. Balance,
  transitions, perspective, and the ending support the concept. For film,
  preserve picture sync and intelligible speech where present.
- **Listening-led revision.** Compare alternatives, seek feedback, and make
  audible improvements. Check the work in the room and on headphones, identify
  translation problems, and address them or explain the remaining limits.
- **Reliable delivery.** Masters and listening copies play correctly, agree
  with the approved specifications, and include the full ending and tails.
  Listen through the exported playback files for missing sounds, unexpected
  silence, distortion, and cut-off endings. Include all media needed to open
  the session. No separate QC report or meter screenshots are required.

so the difference can be heard. Compare versions made during this new final
project, such as the rough mix and the revision after critique.

Finish with what you learned, one next improvement, and the grade you believe
the work earns, supported by the criteria below. Your self-assessment informs
my judgment; it does not determine the grade by itself.

## How the grade is produced

Your approved goals give the project its individual direction. The shared
standards make expectations consistent across different formats and concepts.
An easy goal alone does not establish excellent work; a thoughtful experiment
that partly fails can still demonstrate strong learning and judgment.

| Criterion | Evidence of strong work | Points |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Spatial design and concept | A coherent experience; specific spatial choices support the intended focus and show understanding of spatial perception | /25 |
| Mix quality and development | Clear essential material, controlled balance and transitions, convincing perspective, and a complete approved scope | /20 |
| Experimentation and revision | Alternatives tested, feedback considered, and audible improvement demonstrated through two decision examples, including revision after critique | /20 |
| Individual goals and learning | Meaningful work toward approved artistic and technical goals; honest, specific assessment of results and changes | /15 |
| Delivery and portfolio presentation | Correctly labeled masters and listening copies that play as intended; complete session; clear credits and portfolio description | /20 |

**Total: ___ / 100**

Each category is assessed on the evidence: work that meets the stated
expectations earns B-range credit; consistently accomplished, well-supported
work earns A-range credit. Partial or inconsistent evidence falls in the C
range; substantial gaps fall below that. Scores within each range reflect the
extent and quality of the evidence. Ambition alone does not earn extra credit,
and an explained change of direction is not automatically a deduction.

Discuss goal changes with me and post the agreed update to D2L through the
**December 4 critique**. Later necessary changes should be discussed promptly
and explained in the self-assessment. I assign the final score using these
criteria and explain any difference from your proposed grade. If our judgments
differ by more than a letter step, we meet before the grade is final.
